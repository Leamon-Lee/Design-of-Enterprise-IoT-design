Selected devices: ESP32-C6 \#1/\#2; field sensors and actuators; Aqara C1 feeder; Reolink camera; Zigbee coordinator; Raspberry Pi 5; monitor; switch; router. & Determine the connectivity between selected IoT devices, gateways, routers, etc. & Why do selected devices need to be connected with each other and to the network? & The water-environment detector connects DFR0198, SEN0711 and SEN0189 to ESP32-C6 \#1. The tank-functionality detector connects SEN0257 and SEN0217 to ESP32-C6 \#2 and drives the valve and AC electric water-cycling pump. The two ESP32-C6 nodes and Aqara C1 food feeder use the Zigbee path to the coordinator, while the Reolink camera uses Ethernet. The Raspberry Pi 5 aggregates local status, camera data and feeding events before forwarding selected information to the cloud.\\
\cline{2-4}
 & Determine communication and network technologies required to support connectivity & What are the range and coverage requirements? & Each tank is at most about 30\,m by cable route from the coordinator and the whole farm sits inside one building, so the requirement is short range with block walls and metal racks in the path. DFR0198 uses 1-Wire/GPIO; SEN0711 uses RS-485 Modbus through the DFR0845 isolated converter to UART; SEN0189 and SEN0257 use analog GPIO/ADC with 3.3 V conditioning; and SEN0217 uses conditioned Hall pulses on GPIO. Zigbee at 2.4\,GHz under IEEE 802.15.4 is specified for 10--100\,m, which covers the 30\,m worst case with margin, against 50--100\,m for Wi-Fi and 100\,m per segment for Ethernet. The Reolink camera, Raspberry Pi 5, LAN switch and router use Ethernet over RJ45; HDMI connects the local monitor and USB connects the Zigbee dongle.\\
\cline{3-4}
 &  & What are the power consumption constraints? & The power budget falls into three classes. The ESP32-C6 nodes, sensors, coordinator and Aqara feeder run from low-voltage local supplies, and Zigbee is classed as very low power, whereas the Wi-Fi alternative is classed as high power; that difference, not range, is why the 20 nodes do not use Wi-Fi, because 20 mains-fed stations would still add 20 associations and 20 idle draws to the site AP. The valve uses a 24 VDC supply; the heater and the water-cycling pump are mains-powered and are the two dominant loads; and the Reolink camera draws under 12\,W over PoE. The LAN switch, Raspberry Pi 5, monitor and router require suitable local power. RCD/overload protection and a local backup should preserve the gateway, coordinator and controllers long enough to record alarms and keep bounded local control available.\\
\cline{3-4}
 &  & What is the required data rate (how much data needs to be transmitted over time)? & The measured payload is small: 15 records per tank per minute at about 60 bytes each is roughly 2.4\,kbps for all 20 tanks before MQTT and TLS overhead, which is two orders of magnitude below the 250\,kbps IEEE 802.15.4 ceiling and four below the Wi-Fi figure, so telemetry alone does not separate Zigbee from Wi-Fi. The IP camera is the one bandwidth-heavy device: 8\,MP at 25\,fps needs 4--8\,Mbps, above the Zigbee ceiling, and therefore uses Ethernet to the LAN switch. The Raspberry Pi 5 aggregates and filters the local streams, so the cloud receives selected telemetry, events or processed video rather than requiring every raw local signal to travel continuously over the Internet.\\
\cline{3-4}
 &  & What are the latency requirements (how fast does data need to be transmitted)? & Heater, valve and AC pump decisions use local relay, PWM or driver signals and do not wait for the cloud. Sensor feedback reaches the ESP32-C6 controllers over 1-Wire/GPIO, RS-485/UART, GPIO/ADC or Pulse/GPIO links. Zigbee and Ethernet support gateway monitoring and event delivery, while MQTT/HTTPS cloud communication is used for reporting and alerts rather than immediate safety action.\\
\cline{3-4}
 &  & What are the security and reliability requirements? & Local control should continue when the router or Internet is unavailable. The Raspberry Pi 5 stores data locally when upload is interrupted and the gateway labels or forwards buffered events after reconnection. MQTT/HTTPS traffic to the cloud uses TLS; Zigbee devices require authenticated joining and coordinator access control; and actuator commands require local bounds and interlocks. RS-485 isolation, ADC protection, GPIO level conditioning, RCD/overload protection, cable glands and waterproof enclosures are required.\\
\cline{3-4}
 &  & What are the cost considerations? & ESP32-C6 nodes and short Zigbee links reduce point-to-point wiring inside the device layer. Ethernet is reserved for the Reolink IP camera and the fixed fog/network path, while one Raspberry Pi 5 provides local aggregation, buffering and display output. The selected sensors require signal conditioning and protected enclosures; the AC pump and heater require correctly rated switching and electrical protection. Cloud storage and analytics can be scaled according to selected uploads.\\
\cline{3-4}
 &  & Which communication/network technologies are selected? & Field interfaces are 1-Wire/GPIO for temperature; RS-485 Modbus through DFR0845 and UART for the combined pH/NH3-N sensor; GPIO/ADC for turbidity and pressure-derived level; Pulse/GPIO for flow; and Relay/PWM/Driver for heater, valve and AC pump control. For the links between devices and network devices the decision is Zigbee at 2.4\,GHz under IEEE 802.15.4 for the ESP32-C6 and Aqara feeder, Ethernet at 10/100/1000 Mbps over RJ45 for the Reolink camera and the fog/network path, USB for the coordinator, HDMI for the local monitor, and MQTT/HTTPS over TLS to the cloud platform. Zigbee wins over Wi-Fi because range and rate are satisfied by both while power and channel sharing are not: very low power against high, and 802.15.4 channels 15/20/25 sit in the gaps between Wi-Fi channels 1/6/11 so the two coexist. Ethernet is kept only where the payload or the fixed path justifies cable, and 4G is held as an uplink fallback rather than a device link.\\
