\section{Task 1: Solution Overview}

The Smart Turtle Breeding System (STBS) serves 20 indoor or semi-closed Chinese soft-shell turtle units. Each unit measures water temperature, pH, turbidity, ammonia, level and flow, while zone equipment monitors feed stock, humidity and video. Four local controllers preserve safe rules when the Internet is unavailable. A managed wired network carries controller telemetry and camera streams to an industrial edge computer, which validates data, detects events, buffers outages and performs video inference. Selected telemetry, alarms and evidence clips are sent securely to a cloud service for long-term records, dashboards and notifications. Operators review unit-specific conditions, feeding and nesting evidence, while technicians receive calibration and equipment-fault information. This design refines A1a by selecting deployable device models and separating deterministic local control, bandwidth-intensive edge analytics and cloud reporting \parencite{Prapti2022,Rastegari2023,Munyaev2026}.

\section{Task 2: IoT Devices}

\subsection{Overview and suitability}

The device set favours wired, commercially available instruments because the units are fixed, wet and close to mains power. Industrial 4--20\,mA and RS-485 probes tolerate longer runs and expose diagnostics; local PLCs provide bounded control and manual override. Temperature, pH, turbidity, ammonia, level and flow address high-value aquaculture risks, while a hopper sensor verifies feed availability. PoE cameras support activity and nesting evidence without battery maintenance. The selection links directly to the A1a value proposition: fewer routine rounds, earlier fault detection, more consistent feeding and auditable intervention, without making cloud availability a prerequisite for animal safety.

The selected architecture uses industrial current loops, RS-485/Modbus RTU, pulse/UART, isolated 24\,V control, managed Gigabit Ethernet/PoE and Ethernet-primary/LTE-failover WAN. \subsection{Description of the diagram}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} separates safety control from analytics and reporting. Tank probes and actuator feedback terminate at four Opta PLCs, each responsible for five units. The PLCs execute bounded control locally and exchange states over the control VLAN. Twenty PoE cameras and the PLC uplinks reach a managed switch; the BOXER edge computer consumes separated streams, validates telemetry, runs video inference and buffers outages. The router exposes no field device directly and supplies secure primary-Ethernet/dual-SIM WAN service. The edge publishes validated summaries and events by MQTT/TLS and uploads only selected clips by HTTPS. AWS stores history, routes alarms and serves authenticated dashboards and maintenance exports. Users may acknowledge alarms or propose set-points, but every downward request is logged and revalidated at the edge and PLC. During Internet loss, local control, event storage and the LAN dashboard continue.

\section{Task 6: Conclusion}

The design fulfils the A1a plan by linking continuous water and feed measurements, bounded actuation, video evidence and role-specific alerts across 20 units. Industrial wired probes and zone PLCs improve repeatability and keep essential temperature, circulation and water-exchange rules independent of Internet availability. Edge processing contains video bandwidth, correlates commands with level/flow feedback and preserves a local operating view; cloud services add durable history, comparison, notification and maintenance reporting. The main limitations are probe fouling and calibration effort, the capital and installation cost of industrial sensors and protected wiring, and the need to train and validate vision models under changing light, water and turtle density. Ammonia and video inference are indicators rather than diagnoses, while feeder runtime only estimates delivered mass. A staged pilot on two units should validate thresholds, hydraulic timings, false-alarm rates, model performance and operating procedures before all 20 units are commissioned.
